# Field Note 8 — CVE-class bug: 155 unknown-freshness datasets (T32 at 10× scale)

**Date:** 2026-08-09
**Author:** Redza Halim (with operator)
**Subject:** 46.2% of the trust surface was reporting "unknown" because the probe didn't know which column to extract

## What happened

After the moat-widening dispatch added 169 datasets (commit `3112e9d`),
the live health surface showed **155 of 335 datasets (46.2%) at
`unknown-freshness`** status. The probes were successfully fetching
the data — HTTP 200, record counts populated — but had no extractable
freshness signal.

The cause: the moat-widening dispatch added datasets to
`datapulse.json` but **did not add the matching
`freshness.content-date-field` entries to `scripts/probe-policy.json`**.
Without that entry, the probe doesn't know which JSON column contains
the date.

The bug is exactly **Field Note 3 (T32 fuelprice schema-correction)** at
10× scale. T32 was 1 dataset; this was 155 datasets.

## The fix (commit `7563634`)

A detection script auto-fetched a 1-row sample from each unknown-
freshness URL, scanned JSON keys for date-like columns (`date`,
`date_notif`, `incident_date`, `*_at`, `period`), and added 136 entries
to `probe-policy.json`. 19 were undetectable (logged to
`/tmp/_uf_undetectable.json` for manual review).

Re-probing produced:

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| 155 unknown-freshness | 19 unknown-freshness |
| 98 fresh | 85 fresh |
| 17 aging | 97 aging |
| 59 stale | 128 stale |

**The 80 aging + 69 stale + 6 fresh were datasets we had previously
reported as "we don't know." After the fix, we knew — and most of
them are genuinely stale.** DOSM births data was 2023-07-31; arrivals
2024-10-01. Real data, honestly classified.

## What we learned

1. **Manifest-addition + probe-policy is atomic.** Any future
   dataset-addition dispatch must add probe-policy entries in the
   same operation. The SOP `dispatch-default` now documents this
   (`data + probe-policy is atomic`, 2026-08-09).

2. **The "unknown" status is load-bearing.** It would have been easy
   to silently classify the 155 as `fresh` (because the URLs work)
   or `stale` (because we assumed). We didn't — and the bug being
   visible as `unknown-freshness` is what made it diagnosable. The
   honest taxonomy is the early-warning system.

3. **Redza caught the duplicate.** The hero stat strip on the
   landing page was hardcoded HTML (98/17/59/155) while the lower
   trust summary was JS-rendered from the embedded data (85/97/128/19).
   Two sources of truth on the same page. The fix is now in place:
   the hero renders from `__DATAPULSE_DATA__` like the rest of the
   page. See `df9b13f`.

## Notes for the field

If you build a trust layer for any government open data: **probe-policy
is your second source of truth.** The manifest declares what you
track; the probe-policy declares how you verify it. They must move
together. Silent "unknown" is fine; silent "healthy" is dangerous.

This is the kind of bug that makes your trust layer credible. A
competitor who copy-pastes your manifest but doesn't know about
probe-policy will silently report 46% of their surface as "we don't
know" — which is honest, but they'll never know it's fixable.
